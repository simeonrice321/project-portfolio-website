Gluhareff Pressure Jet

  • The Gluhareff pressure jet is a type of pulsejet that runs on high-pressure propane. The propane entrains air into the engine through a staged inlet by the Venturi effect, from Bernoulli’s principle.
  • I am building this engine to compete in SIOY, BYU’s Student Innovator of the Year competition.
  • The aim is to explore how pyrolysis changes the engine’s efficiency, and to test whether performance increases with greater true airspeed.
  • Pyrolysis is the decomposition of propane into methane, ethylene, and hydrogen under high temperature and pressure.
  • I will gather data on performance and on how pyrolysis is affecting the engine.
  • To see whether efficiency and performance rise because of pyrolysis, I placed two pressure sensors and two thermocouples in the propane fuel line — one of each before and after the heating coil. By watching pressure, fuel temperature, and thrust over time, I can estimate how much of each molecule is being produced and compare that with the increase in thrust.
  • To simulate forward motion, I will place leaf blowers or large fans ahead of the inlets. If extra airflow raises performance, it should show up on the load cell.
  • I intend the engine to reach 100 lbs of thrust, and to gather clear data on pyrolysis and increased airflow.
